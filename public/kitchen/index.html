The mini-hutch — one bay, fully dressed

The mini-hutch · weekend-to-two-days scope · complete in itself

  • Doors off the upper cabinet west of the sink window — hinge mortises filled, holes patched, face frame sanded and refinished so no hardware ghosts remain.
  • Shelf fronts thickened with a 1.5–2" wood face strip, glued and pinned — the single detail that makes exposed shelves read as deliberate.
  • Back of the bay paneled (beadboard) or limewashed; interior painted October Mist so the bay glows against the dark oak.
  • Soffit face above this bay wrapped in wood — the little roof that turns the bay into a hutch. Rest of the soffit painted Kittery Point Green.
  • Warm LED strip (2700K or lower, dimmable) surface-mounted to the soffit underside at its front edge — surface-mounted only, so it can remount onto wood if the dresser wall is ever built. Wire stubbed with generous slack.
  • Stock with weekly things only: everyday mugs, glasses, the jam jar, kid bowls.
  • Alongside: the room paint (walls October Mist, soffit band Kittery Point Green).

The dresser wall — the whole run as one built-in

The dresser wall · carpenter, 1–2 days · contains the mini-hutch

  • The whole west run becomes one tall piece of furniture — closed base, open middle, solid wood cap: a kitchen dresser the length of the wall.
  • Over the sink window: decide by the gap. 4–6"+ between window frame and soffit — clad it as a simple flush valance bridging the window. Tight gap — stop the wood at the cabinets and let the green paint carry the window stretch; the window's light outranks the wood's continuity.
  • Small crown at the ceiling line and a band molding at the cabinet-top joint — the two trim lines that sell the hutch illusion.
  • The LED strip mounts (or remounts) onto the new wood; an electrician formalizes the wiring while the carpenter is there. If clad over the window, the strip continues there as the sink's night task light.
  • Contains the mini-hutch whole: build the one bay first and let the wall grow outward from it, or go straight to the full run — nothing is done twice either way.
